01 — Extensive Form & Strategies
Section 1

Extensive-Form Games

A. Motivation & Core Intuition

In a dynamic game, players move in sequence. A later player can react to earlier actions. With perfect information, the player moving knows the full history: they know exactly which node they are at.

An extensive-form game represents this timing as a tree. Start at the root, follow the chosen branches, and stop at a terminal node, where payoffs are received.

ObjectWhat to identify
Tree $\mathcal T$Decision nodes, branches and terminal nodes; no cycles.
Players $N$ and control $\mathcal P$Who moves at each decision node. $Z_i$ is the set of nodes controlled by player $i$.
Actions $A(z)$The choices available at decision node $z$.
Payoffs $g_i(t)$Player $i$'s payoff at terminal node $t$.
$\Gamma=(\mathcal T,N,\mathcal P,A,g)$
Key Vocabulary
History: actions already taken before a node. Play / path: the sequence actually followed from the root to an endpoint. On path: visited under the chosen strategies. Off path: not visited under those strategies.
Section 2

Pure, Mixed & Behavioral Strategies

A. Pure strategies

A strategy is a complete contingent plan

An action is one choice at one node. A pure strategy specifies an action at every node controlled by the player, including nodes that will not be reached.

$$S_i=\prod_{z\in Z_i}A(z)$$ $$|S_i|=\prod_{z\in Z_i}|A(z)|$$

The product means “choose one action for each node.” With $m$ actions at each of $k$ nodes, there are $m^k$ pure strategies. Two nodes with two actions each give $2\times2=4$ strategies.

▶ Show Proof ▼

A strategy is a tuple with one component per decision node. The first component has $|A(z_1)|$ choices, the second has $|A(z_2)|$, and so on. Every combination defines a plan, so the multiplication rule gives the formula. This counting formula applies to finitely many nodes with finite action sets.

Exam trap: never remove a component just because its node is off path. The plan must say what the player would do there.
B. Random strategies
Mixed strategyBehavioral strategy
Randomizes overComplete pure plansActions at each decision node
InterpretationDraw one plan before play startsMake an independent draw when each node is reached
Strategy set$\Delta(S_i)$$\prod_{z\in Z_i}\Delta(A(z))$

$\Delta(X)$ means the set of probability distributions over $X$. We write $\mu_i$ for a mixed strategy and $\beta_i$ for a behavioral strategy to keep the distinction visible.

$$\mathbb E[g_i]=\sum_{t\text{ terminal}}\Pr(t)\,g_i(t)$$

Under behavioral play, multiply action probabilities along a path to obtain its terminal probability, then multiply by the payoff and sum over endpoints.

Equivalence: in the finite perfect-information games studied here, every mixed strategy has an outcome-equivalent behavioral strategy and conversely. “Equivalent” means the same terminal probabilities against any fixed strategies of the other players; the two objects need not be identical.

The general result requires perfect recall, which these games have.
